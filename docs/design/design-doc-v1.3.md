## 1. Product vision

Build a user-friendly web application in which an individual trader can research markets, record and compare trading ideas, define multi-asset strategies, test them, run them against live data on paper accounts (real accounts later), and review performance.

The product has five work areas, each a primary tab:

1. **Quantitative Research**: price charts, a stock screener, earnings and company data, options figures, a deals tracker and pattern discovery (§14).
2. **Trading Strategy**: the Strategy Lab (build, back test and run strategies), the Theory Library (a shared register of strategy ideas) and Sharing (§6 to §10).
3. **Orders**: orders generated by running strategies and their lifecycle (§12).
4. **Performance**: results by strategy, account, asset and period (§13).
5. **Settings**: profile, data feeds, trading platforms and accounts, AI agents, billing, notifications and security (§15).

Help (the new-user guide) follows Settings; platform admins also see Admin (§5.1). An **Ask AI** side panel is available on every signed-in page (§5.5).

### 1.1 Core product principles

1. **A strategy is not constrained to one asset.** Any condition compares expressions that each name their own instrument, and one Entry or Exit can hold orders for several assets.

   ```text
   Entry condition: SMA(30, PEP) < SMA(30, KO)
   Entry orders:    Buy/long PEP + sell/short KO
   Exit condition:  Spread or risk condition becomes true
   Exit orders:     Close PEP leg + close KO leg
   ```

2. **One definition, one implementation.** Every named computation (a return, a P/E, an implied volatility, a session VWAP) has one definition in the shared metric registry, used by conditions, rankings, the screener and back tests alike (N1, N68, ADR 0040, ADR 0049).
3. **Back test and paper share the rules.** The same evaluator, entry decision, sizing and pre-trade checks run in the back test and the paper runtime (N79, C16).
4. **Missing data is never zero.** An unknown value makes its condition false and leaves a member out of a ranking, with the reason recorded (N1, N35; one stated exception, O19).
5. **Point in time.** Filings, earnings, deals, options signals and model scores are read as they were known at each bar, never later (N34, N57, N137, N240).
6. **Backward compatible.** Saved strategies keep their results: definitions are migrated on read, never rewritten, and a regression corpus of every fixture back test re-runs identically (N15, N84, N214).
7. **Paper only.** Live trading is off by a compile-time constant and database checks on every mode column; no setting turns it on (C27, ADR 0002).

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 In scope and built (paper)

- Responsive web app, desktop first, full screen width (C7); installable as a desktop app window with in-app tabs (§5.4).
- Invite-only sign-up, authentication, profile, member IDs, subscription plans and tenant isolation (§4).
- Theory Library: a shared, numbered register of strategy ideas (§6).
- Strategy Lab: numbered strategies with What to trade, Entry and Exit; groups of assets with filter and ranking; multi-leg orders; schedules; position-value exits; risk sizing (§7, §8).
- Sharing theories and strategies between members (§10).
- On-demand back tests on daily, hourly and 5-minute bars, with results by cycle (§11).
- Paper execution on a built-in paper broker and Alpaca paper accounts; Orders and Performance (§12, §13).
- Data: Alpaca prices and bars, custom REST (JSON) price feeds, CBOE index history (VIX), SEC EDGAR filings, CBOE delayed options chains, EDGAR earnings and deals, an earnings calendar (FMP or Finnhub), FRED rates (§15.2, §16).
- Quantitative Research tools (§14), ML prediction scores (§16.6) and pattern discovery (§14.7).
- AI agents: connect an agent over MCP, an Ask AI panel (§5.5, §15.4).
- Instruments: US stocks and ETFs on NYSE, Nasdaq, NYSE Arca, NYSE American and Cboe BZX (C1); indices such as the VIX as watch-only inputs (N30). Options are inputs to stock and ETF rules only, never traded (N50).

### 3.2 Deferred

- **Real-money trading** (Phase 7, §22.2).
- Trading options, futures, forex or crypto; futures roll automation; options strategy visualisation.
- Quantitative Research: custom formulas, saved screens, sending assets from Research into Strategy Lab, option contracts and futures as screener rows.
- 1-minute bars (O135), point-in-time index membership (O6), per-user ML model series (O151).
- Email or push notifications (§20.3); OAuth for broker connections (§15.3).
- Mobile-native applications.

### 3.3 Non-goals

No high-frequency or ultra-low-latency trading; this is a retail and prosumer workflow. The app is a user-directed tool, not advice. Demo instruments and the demo feed exist only in automated tests (N26, N43, ADR 0046).

## 4. Users, tenancy, accounts and plans

### 4.1 Tenancy

Each person has a private tenant holding their profile, strategies and versions, back tests, data feeds, trading platforms and accounts, orders, fills, positions, performance, subscription and audit events. Nobody may read or change another person's records: application authorisation and Postgres row-level security both enforce it, with composite same-tenant foreign keys (C36, ADR 0003).

Shared by every tenant, written only by the worker: market reference data (instruments), company filings (N39), options recordings (N50), earnings, deals and rates (N135), ML predictions (N239) and the pattern factor store (N261). The Theory Library is a shared register that every member reads and writes (§6).

### 4.2 Sign-up, sign-in and member IDs

- Self-hosted Better Auth with email and password (12 characters minimum). Sessions last 7 days and refresh daily; a person can sign out one or all other devices; a password reset revokes every session (C2, ADR 0004).
- **No email is sent** (ADR 0036). Sign-up needs a single-use **invite code** alone (C5); password resets are done by an operator command. `SIGNUP_MODE=open` exists only for development and tests.
- Every account has a permanent **member ID**, `U0001` onwards in sign-up order, issued by a database trigger and never changed. Settings › Profile shows it; sharing names people by it (N116, ADR 0056).
- Deleting an account needs the password and a typed confirmation. The account is closed at once and purged 30 days later; order and audit records are kept without personal details (C6, ADR 0033). Purging also unlinks every theory its strategies built (N104) and removes its agent activity log (N265).

### 4.3 Platform admins

Admins are rows in `platform_admin`, granted only from the command line with the owner connection (`users:grant-admin`). They see the Admin page (§15.7), share and receive like anyone, may send to "All members", and are marked Admin on what they send; they get no extra read access to other tenants (N25, N124, ADR 0037).

Admins skip two plan limits only: **saved strategies** and **running strategies** (`ADMIN_EXEMPT` in `packages/db/src/entitlements.ts`). Back tests, accounts, feature gates, the paper risk limits and the bars cap at Start apply to them as to anyone. A plan change still pauses strategies over the new plan's running limit; an admin can resume them (N249, N251, ADR 0022 amended).

### 4.4 Plans and billing

| Plan | Saved strategies | Running strategies | Back tests | Accounts                    | Price                               |
| ---- | ---------------- | ------------------ | ---------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Free | 3                | 1                  | 10 a day   | built-in paper account only | $0                                  |
| Pro  | 25               | 5                  | 100 a day  | 5, including Alpaca paper   | $20 a month (test-mode placeholder) |

- The plan catalog is a database table; a limit change takes effect without a release. A missing or malformed limit falls back to the most restrictive value. Real-trading integrations are held false by a database check (C3, ADR 0022).
- Stripe Billing in **test mode only**, with hosted Checkout and the customer portal, so card details never reach the app. Webhooks are signature-checked, stored by event id and the subscription is read back from Stripe, so replays and out-of-order events give the same result; the worker re-syncs each subscription hourly (ADR 0023).
- A failed payment starts a 7-day grace period with notices on days 0, 3 and 6, then the workspace drops to Free. A downgrade or cancel takes effect at the end of the paid period (C4, ADR 0024).
- When a plan shrinks, strategies over its limits are **paused** (newest first, and any on an outside broker account the plan no longer allows), with an audited reason and a notice. Nothing is stopped or liquidated, nothing resumes on its own after an upgrade, and a resume is checked against the current plan.
- Back tests run at most 2 at a time per person. Rate limits: 120 changes a minute per person, 20 back-test starts a minute, 30 order-placing calls a minute; sign-in 10, sign-up 5, reset 3 a minute (C37).

### 4.5 Controlled launch

Invite-only, up to 20 people, paper only, on Railway **staging** (`https://web-staging-737c.up.railway.app`, built from `main` and auto-deployed). Production is not yet created. The risk notice ("paper trading only, simulated orders, not financial advice") shows on sign-up and in every signed-in page's footer; Terms and Privacy are labelled placeholders until legal review (C5, ADR 0026). The pre-launch record is `docs/launch-checks.md`.

## 5. Information architecture and global UI

### 5.1 Navigation

The sidebar, in order:

1. **Quantitative Research** (`/research`), with tools as tabs under its title: Price charts, Stock screener, Deals, Patterns (§14).
2. **Trading Strategy**, showing the count of shares waiting for an answer. Under the page title _Trading Strategy_ sit three tabs: **Strategy Lab** (`/strategy-lab`), **Theory Library** (`/library`) and **Sharing** (`/sharing`, with the same count). The menu item opens the Lab. Pages below a section (a strategy, a theory, a share) keep the tabs above their own heading (N156, N194, O121).
3. **Orders** (`/orders`).
4. **Performance** (`/performance`).
5. **Settings** (`/settings`): Profile, Data feeds, Trading platforms and accounts, AI agents, Subscription and billing, Notifications and security.
6. **Help** (`/help`): the new-user guide video, where later help topics can join (N248, O154).
7. **Admin** (`/admin`), for platform admins only (N25).

The header holds Notifications with an unread count; the sidebar also has **Ask AI** (§5.5) and **Install desktop app** (§5.4).

### 5.2 Global UI principles

- Desktop first, then tablet, with a usable narrow layout. Pages use the full screen width; form rows wrap fields onto a new row rather than shrinking until labels overlap; base text is 17px on screens 1680px and wider (C7).
- Progressive disclosure: common fields first, advanced settings behind expansion; rarely needed sections start collapsed.
- Account mode (**Paper** or **Live**) is shown wherever it matters. Colour is never the only cue: status pairs colour with text and icons.
- Empty states explain the next action; unsaved edits are kept or warned about before navigation.
- Times are stored in UTC and shown in the person's time zone.
- Accessible labels, keyboard navigation, sufficient contrast and visible focus. Esc closes any dropdown and puts the display back as it was (N200, ADR 0071).
- In the strategy editor Entry is tinted green, Exit rose, condition groups blue and order legs amber, always with a matching text heading (C7).
- **Lists sort by any column.** In the Strategy Lab and Theory Library lists, each column header sorts ascending on the first click and descending on the next, with ▲/▼ and `aria-sort`; empty values sort last either way (N167, N168).
- **Filters are compact pills** (TradingView style) in the Theory Library, Strategy Lab and stock screener: a pill opens a small popover and applies at once (no Apply button); a few filters are pinned, the rest wait in "+ Filter"; a set pill shows its value and ×; "Clear filters (n)" removes all; filters live in the address (N195; `components/filters/filter-bar.tsx`, `url-filter-bar.tsx`). New list filters use `FilterBar`.
- The design system is hand-written accessible primitives on Tailwind CSS v4 tokens (light and dark), SVG charts, a nonce-based CSP and no third-party origins (C29, ADR 0005).

### 5.3 Content and interface text

Content (what the person entered and what the app produced: names, descriptions, rules, parameters, results, orders, dates, amounts) looks different from the interface (titles, headings, labels, table headers, buttons, help) (N126 to N134, ADR 0057):

- **Content** uses `text-content`: the content colour `--tm-content` (deep slate-blue `#1e3a5f` in light mode, `#a9c6e8` in dark; at least 9:1 contrast on every surface), medium weight, tabular figures, the system sans. The one primary figure on a card or page uses `text-content-strong`. No webfont (O101).
- **Interface** keeps the neutral ink; field labels are muted, small and semibold; table headers muted uppercase.
- Every table cell is content. Numeric columns and inputs are right-aligned; a money or percentage column names its unit in the header ("Return %", "Net P/L $") and its cells show the bare number (O102). A missing value shows `—`, never `0`. Monospace is for identifiers only (numbers, member IDs, hashes, tickers).
- Typed text in fields is content, so it looks like the saved text; selects keep the interface ink.
- Detail pages use one reading scale: 30px title with the number as a small muted mono line above it, 18px card headings, 14px muted sentence-case labels, 16px values and paragraphs; long text uses `text-prose` (regular weight, proportional figures, 1.625 leading) (N134).
- The content styles sit in the CSS components layer, so a status colour, muted tone or heavier weight on the same element still wins (O105). Components: `Td`/`Th numeric`, `TdNumeric`, `DataValue`, `Stat` in `§tm/ui`.

### 5.4 Desktop app and in-app tabs

- The web app has a manifest (name "Trading Magician", standalone display, start page `/tabs`), icons and an Apple touch icon, so Chrome or Edge **Install** and Safari **Add to Dock** open it in its own window. Manifest and icons load without signing in. There is **no service worker** and nothing is cached offline: prices and orders always come live from the server. It is not a native app (N250, ADR 0089).
- The sidebar's **Install desktop app** button opens the browser's install prompt in Chrome and Edge, and shows the menu steps elsewhere; it is hidden inside the installed app (N252).
- The icon is "Candles + spark" (candlesticks climbing to a spark) on the orange-to-pink "Sunrise" gradient, served from `/icons/v3/`; each icon change moves to a new `/icons/vN/` path so browsers notice it, and a re-install refreshes it (N252, ADR 0089 amended).
- **Tabs inside the app.** The installed app opens into its own tab bar (`/tabs`, `components/tabs/tab-host.tsx`): each tab runs a page with its sidebar and keeps its state; Cmd/Ctrl-click, middle-click or **right-click** on a link to another app page opens a new foreground tab (Shift-right-click shows the usual menu); "+" opens Strategy Lab; × or middle-click closes; up to 12 tabs, restored next time. The listeners run in the frame window's capture phase so a browser add-on cannot take the click. Only the app may frame its pages (`X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN`, `frame-ancestors 'self'`) (N253, N258, N259).

### 5.5 Ask AI panel

An **Ask AI** button in the sidebar opens a panel on the right of every signed-in page (beside the page on wide screens, over it on narrow ones; open or closed is remembered per browser). It reads the screen: page heading and title, focus chips from the address (strategy, theory, pattern, scan, symbol, back test) and the page text, which the person can leave out or trim (N272, ADR 0093).

What the panel does depends on the person's **agent for Ask AI**, chosen in Settings › AI agents (§15.4):

- **Claude (my claude.ai plan)**, the default: one **Ask Claude** button opens `claude.ai/new?q=` with the question and the screen; the answer appears in claude.ai. With an active Claude connection the prompt carries a screen-snapshot code instead of the screen text (N275, ADR 0095, O176).
- **Claude (API key)**, **ChatGPT** or **Another AI service** (OpenAI-compatible): the panel is a chat ("Ask Claude", messages, Send, New chat; Enter sends). Each message goes with the screen to `POST /api/v1/ask-ai/chat`, which runs the agent with the agent tool catalogue (§19.3) as the signed-in person, at most 8 tool rounds, and returns the answer with the tools used shown in plain words; calls are logged in Activity as "Ask AI panel". The chat lasts for the browser tab; 12 messages a minute (N274, ADR 0094).
- **Muse**: one **Ask Muse** button (`muse.ai/?prompt=`); Muse has no API (O174).
- No agent: the panel points to Settings.

**Screen snapshots.** For a connected agent the panel saves a `screen_snapshot` (the owner's only, kept 24 hours, an 8-character code) and the prompt names the code; the agent reads it with the read tool `get_screen_context`. For an agent that is not connected the screen goes into the prompt (page text capped at 2,500 characters in a link, 20,000 in Copy prompt). Nothing is sent to an outside agent until the person sends it there.

### 5.6 Safety-oriented action placement

Start is not a prominent global control: it lives in the strategy page's **Execution** section, after **Back test** (§8.1), which reinforces define → test → review → start. Stop stays easy to reach on a running strategy and in Orders. Live accounts (Phase 7) will need a confirmation summarising strategy, account, market, legs, maximum exposure and existing positions; paper uses a lighter confirmation.

## 6. Theory Library

The Theory Library (`/library`, under Trading Strategy) is a shared register of strategy ideas ("theories"): what each one is, how it trades, the evidence for it, and whether anyone has built it. It is a register, not a builder: it has no condition editors, run controls or order paths. Every signed-in member sees every theory and may register or edit one (N86, N180, ADR 0051). The interface says **theory** throughout; URLs, code names and tables keep "library" and "strategy" (O133).

### 6.1 Theory numbers

- **Format (convention v3).** Record type **T**, a five-digit global sequence, a hyphen, then the asset-class letter and the timeframe letter: `T00001-SD` is theory 1, stocks, days (N107, N181, ADR 0055, ADR 0064).
  - Timeframe, the decision timescale: S seconds, N minutes, H hours, D days, W weeks, M months, Q quarters, Y years.
  - Asset class traded: S stocks, E ETFs, F futures, U funds, O options, B bonds, C crypto, G commodities, A multi-asset.
- **One counter for the whole register**, whatever the letters; past 99999 it grows without re-padding (O82).
- **Issued once, at save, never reused.** The database issues the number in the insert trigger from one locked counter row, in the same transaction as the theory, so concurrent saves get different numbers and a failed save skips none. The app cannot choose a number, registrant or seed key. The Register form shows a live preview ("This will be registered as T00064-SD if saved now"), refreshed as the letters change and every 15 seconds, without reserving it (N89).
- **Never changed.** Number, convention version, letters and registrant are fixed (database triggers; no delete grant). A wrong letter is fixed by retiring the theory and registering it again, linked as related (O66). The only exempt path is the one-off renumbering function `strategy_library_renumber`, a `SECURITY DEFINER` function not granted to the app login (N110).
- **Versioned conventions.** Code tables live in `convention_versions` (label, effective date, layout, minimum digits, the code tables), never edited or deleted. Each theory stores the version that issued its number and is read through it for ever. History: v1 letters-first (`SDS-0001`, 2026-11-07), v2 sequence-first with S (`S00001-SD`, 2026-11-08), v3 with T (2026-11-09) (N87, N88, N107, N181).
- **Former numbers still open the theory.** `former_numbers` keeps every earlier number (unique across the register). A former number redirects the theory page (and its edit page) to the current one and resolves in the API, Library search, the related-theories field and Strategy Lab's theory search. Pages do not print former numbers; the history keeps the renumbering note (N109). Old **S** theory numbers open theories on Library pages; in Strategy Lab an S number is a strategy (§7.1, O134).

### 6.2 The theory record

| Group                | Fields                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity             | Number, name (a duplicate warns but does not block), category (14 primary categories), tags, short description (≤ 280 characters), long description.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Rules in prose       | Assets, data requirements (text), entry and exit (a small Markdown subset, rendered without HTML).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Strategy profile     | Direction (long only, short only, long and short, market neutral, or Not specified); up to 10 parameters (name, value, unit); position sizing, stop loss, rebalance schedule and universe (each a kind from a fixed list plus a detail ≤ 500 characters); maturity (Researched, Back tested, Paper trading, Live; required, default Researched); key risks (≤ 4,000 characters); data needs (daily bars, intraday bars, fundamentals, option chains, earnings consensus, news, level 2 / tick, short borrow, index data). |
| Timing               | Frequency and turnaround as structured durations (value and unit, optional range; frequency may be a count per period; only turnaround may be Unknown).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Evidence             | Prior users, each with an evidence level and a required source ("No known prior use recorded" when none); track record; back test summary; sources (a link or a citation each); related theories (numbers that must exist).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Links and provenance | Built strategies (§7.2); `copied_from` for a shared copy (§10); pattern link for a promoted pattern (§14.7).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

Database checks mirror the domain rules (N90, N112, ADR 0055). A value never shows blank, only "Not specified" (N113).

### 6.3 Pages

- **List.** One row per theory: number, name, short description, asset class, timeframe, category, maturity, built or not, status. Pill filters for asset class, timeframe, category, built, status, direction, maturity, data needs, "Registered by me" and "Shared with me", and a search over number (current or former), name, descriptions, assets and tags. Sorted by number by default; every column header sorts (Maturity by stage, text columns by the words shown; ties keep number order). A tick box on each row and in the header selects theories to share (N113, N121, N125, N168).
- **Theory page.** The number first, what its letters mean under the convention that issued it, the strategy profile (Parameters table, Risk & sizing, Operations, Evidence & maturity with badges, Data needs as chips), provenance with each evidence level, sources, related theories, the strategies that build it, who registered and last changed it, and the history of edits and retirement (N113, N199).
- **Register.** Every required field with the live number preview; the Strategy profile card holds direction, maturity, parameters, method-and-detail pairs, key risks and data needs (N114).
- **Edit.** Every field except the number and convention version, with a required change note; edits are checked against the version the editor opened and kept in an append-only history naming the fields changed, the editor and time. An edit from an older client that leaves the profile fields out keeps what is stored (N91, N114, PR #101).
- **Retire.** With a reason; a retired theory stays listed, badged Retired, with its number, and is never revived (N91).
- **People without names.** Pages show "you", "another member", "a former member" or "the seed catalogue", so no name or email crosses workspaces (N93, O65); the one exception is a share's sender and recipient (§10).

### 6.4 Built status

A theory is **Built** while any of the member's strategies refers to it, and its page lists them as "Built strategies". Any number of strategies, in any member's Lab, may refer to one theory; only a retired theory refuses a new reference (N199, ADR 0071). When the member holding the built link stops referring, the theory reads Not built even if another member's strategies still refer to it, because the server cannot read that workspace on their behalf (O140). Deleting a strategy or an account removes its references, with a history note; the theory and its number stay (N104).

### 6.5 Seed catalogue

31 researched theories are registered in catalogue order right after migrating, in one transaction that checks every number against the seed table and rolls back on any difference; migrating again leaves them alone. The first 18 come from the Strategy Library design document; 19 to 31 are Yong's short-term extension (intraday momentum to triangular arbitrage). Seed prose follows the design documents and published sources; no performance figures are recorded; prior users are named only with a source (N94, N111, O62).

The nine profile fields can be filled by `pnpm --filter §tm/db library:backfill-v2` (dry-run report, then `--apply`), from the seed prose and worked examples only; Yong chose to **skip** this backfill on staging (2026-11-08), so the fields stay empty there until filled by hand (N115).

### 6.6 Library API

`GET/POST /api/v1/library`, `GET/PATCH /api/v1/library/{number}`, `POST /api/v1/library/{number}/retire`, `GET /api/v1/library/next-number` (the preview) and `GET /api/v1/library/conventions`; list filters `side`, `maturity`, `data`. Bodies are validated with the same schemas the database layer checks again (N95, N114).

## 7. Strategy Lab: strategies, numbers and versions

### 7.1 Strategy numbers

- Every strategy has its own number with record type **S**, laid out as theory numbers are (sequence-first, five digits, asset-class then timeframe letters): `S00001-SD`. The Lab's counter is separate from the theory counter, starts at 1 and is shared by all workspaces; numbers are gapless and never reused (N182, N183, ADR 0065, ADR 0066).
- **Issued at creation**, fixtures and empty drafts included. A draft that trades nothing yet gets provisional letters **SD**; the first save with instruments settles the letters once (S00005-SD may become S00005-ED); the digits never change (N186, ADR 0068, O136).
- **Letters.** Asset class: one traded class takes its letter (stocks S, ETFs E); several, A; a stock group counts as stocks; a list group takes its members' classes; watch-only indices do not count. Timeframe: a calendar schedule wins (week W, month M, quarter Q, year Y); otherwise the bars the conditions read (minute bars N, hourly H, weekly W, daily D); mixed or none, D (N101, O73, O79).
- `/strategy-lab/S00032-SD` opens a strategy; `/library/S…` also opens it for its owner. Lab search matches strategy numbers and theories' current and former numbers (N182, N183).

### 7.2 Strategy and theory

A strategy has its own S number and an **optional reference to any theory**. Saving, copying or accepting a strategy never creates a theory. The theory a strategy refers to shows on a line under its name ("Theory T00002-SM Name", or "Theory none referred") and in the list's Theory column (linked, sortable). Double-clicking the line (or F2) opens the theory search; a pick is saved at once; "No theory" removes the reference. A running strategy keeps its theory until stopped (N187, N191, N192, N205, ADR 0072, O138).

Records auto-created from strategies before N205 stay in the register with their links, and each such strategy shows that record as its theory (O141).

### 7.3 The Strategy Lab list

- Columns: Number, Name, Theory, Status, Assets, Version, Account, Latest back test %, Updated; sorted by strategy number ascending by default (page and list API); every header sorts (N167, N187, N188).
- Pill filters for text, status, asset, updated date, archived and Shared; the search finds strategies by S number and by theory number (N103, N121, N195).
- A tick box on each numbered strategy selects it for sharing (N125).
- New strategy (blank or a copy of any strategy, archived included), Make a copy, Rename, Archive and Delete (C8).

Users can create, save, open, rename, copy, archive and delete strategies. **Rename** also works by double-clicking the name (or F2) on the strategy page or in the list: Enter or leaving the field saves, Esc cancels, an empty name is refused; a single click in the list still opens the strategy. Only the name changes, so a running strategy can be renamed (N190, O138).

A **copy** takes the source's rules, orders, timing and risk settings and refers to the same theory; it gets its own S number, and back tests, versions and run history stay behind (C8, N199, N205). **Delete** is a soft delete: the strategy disappears everywhere while its versions and history stay linked to past orders (C8, ADR 0006).

### 7.4 Identity and versions

- A strategy has a stable identity and immutable published versions. Starting a strategy snapshots the draft as a new version unless it equals the current one. After a strategy has run once, every rule change publishes a new version at once. Rules cannot change while it is running; name, description and tags can (C9, ADR 0006).
- Every change carries the row version the client last saw; a stale one is refused, not overwritten.
- Metadata: S number, name, description, tags, owner, status, current version, theory reference, base currency (USD), time zone, timestamps, latest back test summary, legs' accounts.
- The definition is one validated JSON document per version (C34). **Schema 5** is current. Stored drafts and versions are never rewritten: older documents are converted on every read (`parseDefinition` runs `migrateV1` to `migrateV4` in turn) and draft hashes are kept, so nothing reads as changed since its back test. `pnpm --filter §tm/db strategies:migration-report` lists every converted strategy and what each conversion did (N84, N214, N217, N221; ADR 0049, 0073, 0074, 0076).
- Each strategy has a version history page.

### 7.5 Lifecycle

Business status: **Created** (draft; not monitored), **Under testing**, **Actively executing** (the runtime monitors data and may submit orders, on paper) and **Off**.

```text
Created <--> Under testing
Under testing --> Actively executing
Actively executing --> Off
Off --> Under testing
Off --> Actively executing, after validation and confirmation
```

Runtime health is separate from status: Starting, Monitoring, Waiting for entry, Entry pending, Position open, Waiting for exit, Exit pending, Paused, Error, Stopped. A running strategy can also be **paused** (by the person, a plan change or an automatic risk pause) and resumed on purpose (§12.4).

## 8. Strategy definition (the builder)

### 8.1 The strategy page

The strategy page is one full-width column in three numbered sections, each with a large heading and one line on what it holds (N171, ADR 0062, O129):

1. **Strategy**: What to trade, Entry, Exit, entry groups, Order handling, Risk limits, Validation, in that order. Order handling and risk limits sit here because the back test simulates them (O128); Validation is last because it checks everything above it and gates both the back test and Start (N173, O130).
2. **Back test**: one "Run a back test" card (end date and period, members, capital, costs and fills, Run, the latest back test against the current draft, a running test and the history), followed by the results (§11) (N175).
3. **Execution**: a **Ready to start** card (status, saved, validation and latest back test, each with a mark, and the status buttons with the start and stop dialogs); an **On paper** card (how legs are sent, what happens if a leg fails, accounts, latest back test, links to Orders and Performance filtered to this strategy); then Versions and status history. While the strategy runs the first card is titled **Running on paper** and holds Stop (N176).

The sticky save bar has a jump link per section with its state: Strategy (unsaved changes, or the problem and warning counts), Back test (not tested, matches latest, changed since latest), Execution (status). Sections have the ids `strategy`, `back-test` and `execution` (N172, N177).

The header shows the S number, the name (double-click to rename) and the theory line (§7.2).

### 8.2 What to trade

The **What to trade** card is the only place instruments are chosen (`trade` in the definition; N206, ADR 0073):

- **Specific assets** (`{kind: 'assets'}`): any stocks or ETFs, named by the Entry legs. Two or more are entered and exited together as one position (pairs, hedges) (O142).
- **A group of assets** (`{kind: 'group', universe, filter?, ranking?, splitTotal?}`):
  - **Group (members).** A built-in stock group, or the person's own list of up to 600 instruments. Built-in groups: S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, Dow 30, S&P MidCap 400, S&P SmallCap 600 and the eleven S&P 500 sectors; Russell 1000 and 2000 are defined but hidden until their member lists are loaded (N231, ADR 0080). Members are **today's** members for the whole test period, so back tests carry survivorship bias and say so (N14, O6).
  - **Filter** (optional): a condition on each member, where "this stock" means the member being checked.
  - **Ranking** (optional): a registry metric, highest or lowest first, top N or top N % (rounded down, at least 1). Ties break by symbol A to Z.
  - Filter and ranking run in that order at each **Check Entry**; there is no separate refresh schedule (N207, O143).
  - **Without a ranking**, every member that passes is a candidate, in symbol order, up to **Max instruments held**, which such a group must set (`group_needs_max_held`, O147).
  - **Amount per pick.** The group's Entry leg amount is per pick ("$2,000 for each pick") by default; "A total, split equally across the picks" (`splitTotal`) is an option (N212, O146).
  - **Sell a pick when it leaves the group** (the `leftGroup` rule): a holding that no longer passes the filter or falls out of the ranking is sold at the next Entry check (exit reason "Left the group: no longer selected"). Without it, each pick is kept until its own Exit when, so more than N may be held, each new pick still entering at its own amount; the strategy's risk limits still apply (N211, N222, O116).
- An index (the VIX) can be read in conditions but never traded: a leg, list or group refuses it with a note (N30, N193).

**Any symbol, anywhere.** Every instrument picker ends with "Another stock, ETF or index…", which opens a symbol box; a ticker or company name typed there is looked up through the person's data feed and joins every picker. Nothing has to be added beforehand (N193, ADR 0069).

**Kept as saved.** Strategies saved before the three-part model that used **per-leg conditions** or a **Selection group of these legs** (ranked legs) keep running exactly as saved (`entry.rankedLegs`, leg conditions), shown with a note in What to trade; the builder no longer offers them for new strategies (N213, O148).

### 8.3 Entry

- **Enter when**: one condition tree. With a group it is checked for each candidate and may use "this stock"; a condition on a named symbol gates every candidate (a regime filter such as "SPY above its 200-day average"). With specific assets it decides when the position opens. An empty Enter when is allowed for a group (every check rebalances) and for timed entries (§8.10); otherwise Entry needs at least one condition (`empty_condition`) (N210, N29).
- **Check Entry**: when Enter when, the filter and the ranking are evaluated (§8.4).
- **Fire when** (§9.2).
- **Legs**: the Entry orders (§8.8).
- **Risk distance**: one ATR period and multiple per Entry, entered once in the Entry card, read by risk sizing and by the ATR exit; the ATR exit's own distance is an explicit override (N67, O49).
- **Direction** is derived from the legs (Buy is long, Sell short is short) and shown, never stored (N77, O58).
- **Entry groups.** A strategy may have up to three more entry groups, each with a name and its own Entry and Exit (for example a long breakout and a short breakdown). Each further group shows one summary row (name, direction, leg counts) that opens to its cards. A new group starts in the opposite direction with group 1's legs mirrored. The strategy holds **one position at a time** and never flips it: groups are checked in order and one enters only while no position of any group is open (first to qualify wins a tie). Groups need re-entry "One cycle at a time" (`groups_single_position`) and specific assets (`groups_fixed_legs_only`); a stated direction must match the legs (`direction_side_mismatch`) (N41, N83, ADR 0044).

### 8.4 Check Entry and Check Exit schedules

Entry and Exit each have a schedule (`entry.schedule`, `exit.schedule`), edited as three boxes (N208, N209, N227, ADR 0078):

| Box      | Choices                                                                                         |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Which    | Every bar, 1st bar, 2nd bar, Last bar, Before the open                                          |
| Bar size | 1 minute, 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day                                                  |
| Days     | Every trading day, or the last trading day of each week, month, quarter or year (NYSE calendar) |

- "Every 1-day bar" is once a day after the close; on daily bars 1st, 2nd and Last mean the same.
- **Before the open** is meant to check one bar (at most an hour) before 09:30 in paper runs and at the prior close in back tests: same data, same fill at the open (Yong's reading of "before", 2026-11-11). **Known deviation (N227, not yet fixed):** the paper runtime has no upper bound on that window (`apps/worker/src/runtime.ts`, the `beforeOpen` branch fires whenever data exists), so a runtime that starts mid-session still fires the check that day. Back tests behave as designed. A code fix is owed (§25).
- Every bar of a size can be limited to calendar days (for example every hourly bar on month-end days).
- Back tests check at the first run bar ending at least that long after the open and warn when the run steps on coarser bars; paper runs check when that bar is in, or by the clock when the rules read only daily bars (N226).
- Picking 1st, 2nd or Last from "every bar the run steps on" or on daily bars sets the bar size to 5 minutes in one step. "Last" only times when Exit when is checked; it closes nothing by itself (N257; flatten is §8.6).
- **No 1-minute feed exists yet**, so 1-minute checks run on 5-minute bars, with a back-test warning (O135).
- Saved schedules (`frequency`, `at`, the N184 bar cadences) read as the same checks through `scheduleFrom`; nothing is rewritten.
- **Absent Check Exit** means every bar the run steps on, which reproduces every saved strategy's results. A new group gets Check Exit = Check Entry, or once a day when Check Entry is a week, month, quarter or year end (O145).
- Exit when is checked only when Check Exit is due; positions, fills and the stops' best closes keep updating on every bar. Back tests warn when Check Exit is finer than the bars (`exit_schedule_finer_than_bars`) or is a calendar period (`exit_schedule_calendar`).
- Back test results show both schedules in words (N220, N227).

### 8.5 Exit

- **Exit when**: one condition tree, combined by OR across its top-level parts. It may read position values (§8.7), which is how every stop and holding-period exit is expressed: the builder's buttons under Exit when ("Add to Exit when: + Trailing stop, + Holding period, + ATR stop, + Expected move") add the matching condition by OR (N217, N221, N222, ADR 0074, ADR 0076).
- **Check Exit** (§8.4).
- **Legs**: the Exit orders (§8.8), with the **Mirror Entry legs** switch.
- A back test names which part of Exit when held: "Why it exited: Exit condition (trailing stop)" (N223).
- Exit when may be empty when Left the group or Flatten at session end closes positions (N256).
- **Exit rules kept as saved.** Strategies saved with the earlier exit rules (time stop, ATR stop, expected-move exit, trailing stop) are converted on read into the equivalent Exit when conditions where the results stay identical (Exit's Fire when "while true" with no confirmation or cooldown); elsewhere (another Fire when, or per-leg / ranked-leg strategies) the rules are kept as saved, listed under Exit when, and keep their own exit labels. Exit holds up to 6 rules (N211, N217, N221).

### 8.6 Order handling

- **Multi-leg policy.** Submit concurrently (default), sequentially in a chosen order, or the next leg only after the prior fill. A leg rejected or still unfilled after the **60-second leg timeout** cancels the remaining legs and flags the imbalance (default); the other choices flatten what filled, or leave it and alert. The UI never implies atomic fills. The timeout shows only when a phase sends several legs (C13, N78).
- **When the market is closed**: queue to the next regular NYSE session (default; rule-based NYSE calendar with holidays, weekend observance, 1 pm early closes and announced closures), cancel, or extended hours if the account supports them. This one strategy setting decides; legs have no extended-hours box of their own (C14, N75, ADR 0032).
- **Re-entry**: one cycle at a time (default), pyramiding up to a cap, or independent cycles (C15).
- **Exit waits for the full Entry fill** by default; a partly filled Entry leg at the timeout keeps the filled part open and Exit sizes to what is held (C13).
- **Allow timed entries without conditions** (§8.10).
- **Flatten at session end** (`flattenAtSessionEnd`, off by default; intraday strategies only): at the last bar of each NYSE session (13:00 on early closes), after Exit when, whatever a cycle holds is closed and its working orders canceled; exit reason "Session end". Back tests close at that bar's close less slippage; paper flattens with market orders on its passes in the session's last five minutes (from 15:55, or 12:55). Yong accepted this asymmetry as designed (O156). A daily-only strategy refuses it ("Flatten at session end needs an intraday bar size") (N256, ADR 0090).

### 8.7 Conditions and expressions

A condition compares two independently configured expressions: `left  operator  right`. Conditions nest in **All of these** / **Any of these** groups with NOT; the builder makes grouping visible and lets the person add, remove, reorder and duplicate conditions, groups and legs. Duplicating gives the copy and everything inside it new ids, and a duplicated leg is named "<leg> copy", "<leg> copy 2" (N154, ADR 0060). **Each side picks its own instrument**; nothing inherits a global symbol. When What to trade names a stock or ETF, new conditions, values and legs start on it (N273).

**Operators:** `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=`, `=`, `!=`; crosses above, crosses below; between, outside; is available, is stale; and **Not needed** (`is_true`) for yes/no metrics, which holds when the metric says yes and is unknown without data (`not_needed_needs_yes_no` elsewhere). A crossing is true on one bar only (N216).

**Value types** (the builder's Type list):

#### 8.7.2 Position values

Allowed only in Exit when and Exit order limit or stop prices, only for instruments an Entry leg trades, and not with crossing operators. With no Entry fill yet a position value is unknown, so its condition does not hold (C10, ADR 0027).

| Position value                                     | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Entry price, Gain %, Unrealized P&L, Quantity      | Average Entry fill; gain positive when winning (also for shorts); marked at the latest Check Exit close.                                                                                                      |
| Off best close since entry % (`off_peak_pct`)      | How far the close is below the best close since entry (above the lowest, for a short). "> 5" is a 5% trailing stop. The best close starts at the entry price.                                                 |
| Trading days held (`days_held`)                    | Trading days from the day Entry first filled, that day included; "≥ 5" holds five closes. Counts trading days on intraday bars too.                                                                           |
| Minutes held (`minutes_held`)                      | Whole minutes from the earliest Entry fill to the evaluation (the bar's end in back tests, the pass time on paper). A 09:40 fill makes "≥ 30" first true at the bar ending 10:10.                             |
| Below entry, in ATRs at entry (`atrs_below_entry`) | (Entry − close) ÷ ATR, with the ATR (daily, Wilder, its own period) fixed when Entry is decided; "> 2" is a 2-ATR stop.                                                                                       |
| Move since entry, in 1-day / 5-day expected moves  | The move since entry in units of the first Entry leg's options expected move captured when Entry is decided; positive in the holding's favour, so "> 1" and "< −1" are the two sides. Needs the options feed. |

Closes for these values are read at the **Check Exit bar size** (5-minute closes for every 5-minute or 1-minute check, hourly for hourly, daily otherwise; the ATR stays daily), and **no close from before Entry first filled** is read (N215, N225, O144). The cycle keeps the best close, the ATR and the expected move with it, in back tests and on paper (N148).

### 8.8 Orders (legs)

Entry and Exit each hold order legs. Every leg defines its instrument (or "each pick" for a group), side (buy, sell, sell short, buy to cover, close), quantity, order type, limit or stop expression, time in force, trading account and timing (§8.9).

- **Quantity types**: units; cash amount; % of equity; **Risk**: an amount (dollars or % of equity) over a distance (the leg's stop price, or the Entry's ATR distance) (N73). With the ATR distance: quantity = floor((equity × risk % ÷ 100) ÷ (ATR × multiple)), using equity to the cent and the daily Wilder ATR to 4 decimals as of the decision bar, exactly as the results show them. No ATR or a quantity of 0 places nothing, with the reason recorded. The quantity is then lowered to fit buying power, the maximum order size and the maximum exposure, naming the limit; sizing never overrides a limit. Risk sizing is for Entry legs (`risk_atr_entry_only`) (N42, N67).
- **Rounding**: **Up, at least 1 unit** (default for new legs: any size above zero buys at least one unit), down, or to nearest. No fractional shares. A saved leg with no rounding reads as down (N273, C16).
- **Order types**: Entry legs market, limit, stop or stop-limit; **Exit legs market, limit or close** (stops are expressed in Exit when; saved stop Exit legs keep working as saved) (N80, O51).
- **Time in force**: day, GTC, IOC, FOK.
- **Accounts**: a new leg starts on a broker's paper account (Alpaca paper) when one is connected, the default first, otherwise on the built-in paper account; never a live account (N28). Capabilities (order types, time in force, sides, extended hours, short selling, shortability) drive validation (C20).
- **Mirror Entry legs** (on by default for new strategies): each Entry leg has an Exit leg on the same instrument and account with the opposite side and quantity, at market, regenerated on every Entry edit and not editable. A leg sized by cash, % or risk becomes a Close. Off: Exit legs are built by hand (N74).
- A group has one Entry leg trading each pick and Exit legs that close it (N2).

### 8.9 Timing

Each leg has one timing mode: **immediate** (when its phase qualifies), **exact date/time** (zoned; a time without a zone is invalid; a past time is refused), or **relative delay** (an amount in seconds, minutes, hours or days after a durable reference event: Entry trigger, Entry submitted, partly filled, fully filled, position opened, or another named leg submitted or filled). Relative timing references a durable event id, and scheduled actions survive restarts without duplicate submission. With a group (or kept-as-saved per-leg strategies) only immediate and phase-relative timing are offered (N76).

### 8.10 Timed entries without conditions

"Allow timed entries without conditions" (in Order handling) lets a timed leg go on its timing alone, so an Entry leg at an exact time opens a cycle by itself; off, timed legs also wait for their phase's conditions. Not available with a group (`slots_timing_only`) (N79).

### 8.11 Risk limits

Required before Start and simulated in back tests: max order notional, max strategy exposure, max active orders, daily loss limit and price collar. Optional: maximum drawdown and **Maximum instruments held** (`maxInstrumentsHeld`, instruments with a position). Paper defaults: $10,000 per order, $25,000 strategy exposure, 10 active orders, $1,000 daily loss, 5% price collar, 60 s stale data. No leverage: exposure may not exceed strategy equity (C16, N82). The checks themselves are in §12.3.

### 8.12 Validation

The draft always saves; validation problems block back tests and Start, and warnings are acknowledged. Blocking checks include: an invalid, removed or untradable instrument (`removed_instrument`, `instrument_not_tradable`); an unsupported indicator or parameters; missing feed coverage (`missing_company_feed`, `missing_options_feed`); a missing, inactive or incapable account (order type, time in force, short selling, `not_shortable`); invalid quantities, timestamps or relative references; circular timing; an Exit that would sell without a long or cover without a short; position values outside Exit; missing required risk limits; a crossing with confirmation bars (`cross_with_confirmation`); a group without a ranking and without Max instruments held; Left the group without a group or twice; "Not needed" on a non-yes/no metric; Flatten at session end on a daily-only strategy; Bars ago on a session value; group and direction rules (§8.3). Warnings include non-atomic multi-leg execution, extended-hours limits, a breakout window that includes the current bar, session values without intraday series, stale or missing back tests, coarser back-test bars than the checks, and one ATR distance watching the first leg of several (C16, N29, N40, N56, N71, N207, N211, N216, N254, N256, N260).

### 8.13 Builder defaults

New strategies, entry groups and assets start with Fire when **Immediately**, legs rounding **up**, new conditions and legs on the What to trade asset, and Mirror Entry legs on. Saved strategies keep their stored choices (N273).

## 9. Evaluation and the cycle lifecycle

### 9.1 Evaluation pass

For each due check: resolve every expression's data; check freshness and lookback; compute indicators without lookahead; evaluate comparisons and the condition tree; apply Fire when; record a durable trigger event once per bar, so a retried pass never fires twice (C27).

### 9.2 Fire when

One control sets the trigger mode and confirmation (N70, N71, N273):

- **Immediately** (default for new strategies): fires on every check the condition is true, with no confirmation or cooldown (stored as "while true", 0 and 0).
- **As soon as it becomes true**: only when false turns true.
- **After it has been true for N bars**.
- **Repeatedly while true**, with a cooldown counted in bars (evaluations): a cooldown of N lets it fire again N evaluations after it last fired.
- **Once per cycle**: does not re-enter until the cycle exits; re-armed when the cycle closes or is aborted, in back tests and on paper. Pausing keeps the trigger; stopping and starting a strategy starts a fresh one (O53).

Exit when on a group fires on every check it is true (no trigger) (N72).

### 9.3 Cycle lifecycle

```text
Waiting for Entry
--> Entry qualifies at a Check Entry
--> Entry order intents created from the legs, validated, submitted
--> Acknowledgements and fills tracked; position opens
--> Exit monitoring begins (default: after the full Entry fill)
--> Exit when holds at a Check Exit (or Left the group, or session end)
--> Exit orders created, submitted and reconciled
--> Cycle closes; performance calculated
```

One entry decision (`planEntry`) serves back tests and paper runs (N79). Each strategy holds one open cycle at a time by default (C15).

### 9.4 Groups

At each Check Entry every member's filter and ranking metric are computed as of that bar's close; members failing the filter, with an unknown filter or without a metric value are left out with the reason recorded. Selected members not held are entered; held members no longer selected are sold if Left the group is on (once their Entry has filled); held members still selected are kept without selling and buying back. Fewer passing members than N: only those are held; none: cash. Each pick runs its own cycle (N3, N7, N211).

## 10. Sharing

Members can share theories and strategies with each other (`/sharing`, under Trading Strategy; ADR 0056).

- **What a share is.** A snapshot of what was shared (the theory, and for a strategy share the strategy), with the sender and recipient as named then (name and member ID), a note of up to 500 characters and a **30-day expiry**. The preview and the copy read only the snapshot; a copy never follows later changes, and sharing again makes a new copy (N117, O98).
- **Who.** Any member may share a registered theory or a numbered strategy of their own (not a fixture), never with themselves or a locked or deleted account. The admin can pick "All members". **No limits** on people per send, items per send, shares pending or sends an hour (O104).
- **Several at once.** Tick boxes on the Library and Lab lists select items; "Share selected (n)" sends each item to each person as its own share, all or nothing, and a refusal names the number (N125).
- **Answers.** A pending share is accepted or declined by the recipient, withdrawn by the sender, or expires; only pending changes, once; a repeated accept returns the first copy. "Shared with me" answers several at once ("Accept selected", "Decline selected" after a confirm, "Accept all"), each on its own with a running count, listing any that failed and why (N120, N155, O120).
- **Accepting a theory** registers a new theory by the recipient with the next T number, every person-written field (maturity as written), the tag `shared`, `copied_from` and a history note naming the source and the sender's member ID (N118, O97).
- **Accepting a strategy** creates it in the recipient's Lab (Created, no versions, back tests or runs) with its own S number, referring to the same theory, with every leg on the recipient's paper account (a placeholder, and a prompt, when they have none) (N119, N205, O96).
- **Provenance.** A received copy shows "Shared by name (U…) on date, from …" to the share's two people and "Copied from …" to others; the Lab has a Shared badge and filter (N121, O95).
- **Notifications.** `share_received`, `share_accepted`, `share_declined`, one per share; the Trading Strategy menu item and the Sharing tab count pending shares (N122, N156). The Sharing page lists the latest 1,000 shares.

## 11. Back test

A back test belongs to the selected strategy and runs only when the person chooses **Run back test** (the former "Sample trigger check"). Results are estimates, not guarantees, and the assumptions are shown beside them.

### 11.1 Inputs

- **Strategy version**: the saved draft at the time of the run.
- **End date and look-back period.** An end date (today by default) and a period back from it: 1 or 2 weeks; 1, 3 or 6 months; 1, 2, 3 or 5 years. Periods longer than the strategy's bar size allows are greyed out. A run ending today reads up to the previous close. Runs store from, to and the period (N234, ADR 0082).
- **Bar size from the strategy.** There is no Bars choice: Check Entry, Check Exit and the conditions set it (5-minute when anything reads bars under an hour, hourly for hourly or 4-hour, daily otherwise), shown in words. The server sets it from the saved rules whatever a request sends (N233). Longest ranges: 5 years on daily bars, 1 year on hourly, **31 days on 5-minute** (O132). There are no tick or 1-minute runs.
- **Members.** For a group, the whole group (default) or only some members, typed as symbols or clicked from the list; the worker narrows the group before loading bars, so filter, ranking and Left the group read only those (N235).
- **Capital and costs.** Initial capital; market orders fill at the next bar's open plus **5 bps** slippage; limits and stops fill when a later bar trades through them; no commission by default (per-share with a minimum available); at most 10% of a bar's volume per fill; **1% a year** short-borrow cost charged at each close, kept for shorts (O26). No leverage; margin calls and forced buy-ins are not modelled (C18, N41).
- **Data.** Bars through the person's routed price feed (or the admin's shared feeds, §15.2). Custom-feed bars are used as the provider gives them (the app's split and dividend setting is not applied, and results say so) (N22).
- Runs are limited by plan (§4.4) and to 2 at a time per person.

### 11.2 Engine

- The **same rule definitions, entry decision, sizing and pre-trade checks** as paper execution; no lookahead; timestamps aligned across assets; multi-leg timing, partial fills and sequencing simulated; each result records the strategy version, data versions, assumptions and **engine version** (bumped whenever simulation semantics change).
- Asynchronous, with status queued, running, completed, failed or canceled; runs live in Postgres queue tables with leases (§17.1). The simulation starts on a **10-minute lease** because it cannot renew while it runs (N232).
- **Sized up front.** A run whose price data would pass about **1.2 million bars** is refused at start with the size and what drives it; the worker checks again before loading any bar. Stale data is checked once per step, not once per member (S&P 500 daily runs take seconds) (N232, ADR 0081).
- **Bars that fail.** A failed run names the instrument and the feed's own reason, never a key ("Bars for SPY could not be loaded: Alpaca is over its request limit for this key"). A group member whose bars fail is left out with a warning naming it; any other instrument's failure stops the run (N44).
- **Rate limits.** Back tests wait out a feed's request limit (Retry-After or reset time, up to 8 tries per request) instead of failing. Their bar loads are background work inside each Alpaca key's request budget, with a floor of 10 background requests a minute so a 5-minute back test still progresses while paper strategies use the key (N44, N178, ADR 0052 amended).
- **Point-in-time data.** Filings are synced before the run (a company is fetched again after a day) and read as filed (N37). Options signals: a daily bar reads that day's signal, an intraday bar the previous session's; a run entirely before an underlying's first signal fails by name ("No options data for SPY before …"), and rebuilt history is approximate and counted (N57). Earnings, deals and the T-bill rate are read as of each bar; a run ending before the first record fails by name (N143). Before the earnings calendar first listed a stock, the Earnings window's days before a release use the actual EDGAR release date (O137, open). Model scores load like options signals, outside the bar plan; a run ending before a model's first score is refused, one starting before it warns (N240).
- **Index bars** (VIX) line up with NYSE sessions and never set the back-test clock (N32).
- **Survivorship.** Built-in groups use today's members; members not yet known to the app are registered from the feed's asset list, and any it cannot find are named (N14).

### 11.3 Results

The results sit under the Back test section of the strategy page (O125):

- **Run summary** (collapsed; its title shows the net return): version, dates and bars, finish time, engine and data versions, the figure tiles (net and gross return, maximum drawdown, win rate, profit factor, Sharpe-like ratio with its method, cycles and orders), equity and exposure charts, and the assumptions, including what each position value in Exit when means. Failed and canceled runs show their message (N170, N223).
- **Cycles** (the main view): one row per cycle (cycle number, instruments with direction and group, Entry and Exit fill times and prices, days held, exit reason, net P/L, return), including cycles whose Entry never filled or was still pending. Filters (All, Won, Lost, Open or not filled; exit reason; instrument), sort (cycle number, net P/L, return worst first, days held), 25 rows at a time, cycle 1 first and none open unless `?cycle=N` names one (N157, N161, O122, O123, O127).
- **The cycle card**, opened inline under its row, follows the strategy's three parts (N158 to N160, N218 to N220, N224, N241):
  - **What to trade** across the top: the assets, or for a group the universe, when the stock was picked, how many members were checked, the filter as it read for this stock (passed by N of M) and the ranking (value, order, rank, top N kept).
  - **Entry beside Exit.** Each shows its schedule ("Check Entry …", "Check Exit …" or "Every bar the run stepped on"), its signal (the bar that fired, each condition as a tree, "All of these" / "Any of these" with its result, and under each comparison the data it read with values, the bar each came from when not the bar checked, and a **Why** line such as "52.96 ≥ 52.91"; earnings, deal, options and model records named in words, e.g. "ML score 0.83 — model mf-ranker-ridge, run 14, trained through Aug 29, 2026, score dated Sep 30, 2026"), then **Key data** (checks since the previous signal, how many were true, false or without data, when the conditions first turned true and when they fired; timing from signal close to fill; for each order the last close against the fill, slippage, commission and best price while working), then one box per leg (order, decided, could fill, filled, result and why). Risk sizing ("Sized from risk % of equity") sits on the Entry side.
  - **Legs**: each instrument's Entry legs against its Exit legs with signed quantity, average fills, gross P/L, costs and net P/L, with a cycle total; a flatten after a failed leg is named.
  - **While open** (collapsed): the Exit checks of that cycle and the stop figures in force (ATR or expected move at entry, best close, close checked).
  - **Why it exited** names the part of Exit when that held (e.g. "Exit condition (trailing stop)"), "Left the group: no longer selected", "Session end", or a kept-as-saved rule's own label.
  - Previous and Next move between cycles.
- **Signals that opened nothing**: a line counting Entry firings that did not open a cycle, by reason (a cycle already open, daily loss limit, drawdown) (N163).
- **Rules and full logs** (collapsed; its title shows the warning count): Entry and Exit conditions with their counts and bar-by-bar logs (up to 2,500 bars per phase; a condition with no recorded check shows `—`), every order, selection rounds for groups (each evaluation's ranking table, up to 400 rounds, downloadable as CSV), contribution by instrument, and warnings (C19, N12, N164, O124).
- **Downloads**: "Download cycles and legs (CSV)" (one row per instrument per cycle) and the trades CSV (with direction, group, sizing, stop figures and `exit_reason`) (N12, N42, N165).
- The engine stores each cycle's records (cycle numbers on Entry bars, per-instrument P/L, stop checks, the pick, `ConditionCheck.inputs`) in the run's result, with the run's `plan` (what it traded and both schedules) so the card reads without the version; none of this changes a decision and the regression fingerprints leave it out. Runs from before show what they recorded, with a note to run again (N166, N219, N220).

Marking cycles on the equity chart is a later follow-up (O126).

### 11.4 Back test and Start

A back test is **not required to start on paper**: a missing or out-of-date one is a warning the person acknowledges in the Ready to start card, which shows the latest back test against the current rules (C18, N176). Live activation policy is a Phase 7 decision.

## 12. Paper execution and Orders

### 12.1 Start and stop

- **Start** (from the Execution section) validates the strategy, snapshots the version, creates a runtime and links to Orders with the new run highlighted. Start refuses a strategy whose bars would exceed **400,000** (`MAX_PAPER_BARS`), naming what drives the size (N245). Built-in groups and lists of any length can run on paper (N13, N245). Removing the old S&P 500 back-test-only cap still needs Yong's confirmation (O17, open, §25).
- **Stop** stops new strategy orders. It offers two separate choices, both off by default: cancel working orders, and close positions. It never silently cancels or liquidates (C33).
- **Kill switch** (Orders): pauses every running strategy and cancels nothing; cancel-all is a second, separately confirmed action; releasing the switch resumes nothing, and each strategy is resumed on purpose.
- **Closing a position by hand** creates a user-sourced market order and is allowed only once the strategy is stopped, because a running strategy's Exit owns its positions.

### 12.2 The runtime

- A runtime pass is one tenant transaction: lock the paper broker, advance it, send cancels, submit ready intents, sync broker updates, reload and plan. Any failure rolls the whole pass back and the next pass repeats it. A lease ensures one worker owns a runtime (C27).
- **Bars between passes.** A runtime loads each series once, then asks the feed only when a bar of that size can have ended (plus 20 s, or 5 minutes for daily bars). Alpaca answers up to 100 symbols per request, so a whole group takes a few requests. Series are shared by runtimes on the same feeds and dropped 15 minutes after the last reader. Built-in group members are looked up once a day; events, filings, options signals and model scores reload on a new bar or after a minute (N245, ADR 0087).
- A running strategy reads the latest stored options signal on each pass, so an options condition changes at most once a day (N57). A pass that cannot get filings leaves fundamental metrics unknown and logs it (N37).
- Paper fills come from the built-in paper broker (simulated, at the person's feed prices, on real US listings; the "Built-in paper broker" / "Paper account") or from Alpaca's paper API. Broker updates are read by polling (order list and fill activities by cursor) plus reconciliation; no broker streams or webhooks (C32).

### 12.3 Pre-trade risk checks

Risk checks run immediately before each submission, by the same function in back tests and on paper, and every check's limit and value are stored on the order intent. Order: stale data; active orders; then for orders that open or add risk: order size, strategy exposure, buying power (no leverage), daily loss, drawdown and instruments held; then the price collar. Closing orders skip the exposure checks. The stale-price watch covers only members held or with a working order, but a stale price still refuses a new pick (C16, N245).

Not yet built and required before real trading: account-level, per-symbol and per-asset-class limits (v1.1 deviation D4).

### 12.4 Automatic pauses

A strategy pauses itself after 3 rejected or failed orders in a row since the last resume, prices stale 60 s past the threshold, the broker not answering for 60 s, a reconciliation mismatch, or a daily loss or drawdown hit. Each pause is audited and notified; resolving an incident never resumes a strategy (C17).

### 12.5 Order intents and states

When a trigger and timing qualify, each leg becomes an immutable **order intent** recording user, strategy, version, cycle, phase, leg, trigger event and values, timing event, account, instrument, requested fields, risk-check result and an idempotency key (`tm1-` plus 32 hex characters from runtime, cycle, phase, leg and attempt, sent as the broker's client order id). Broker ids, acknowledgements, updates, fills, fees and rejection reasons are appended, never overwritten (C27, ADR 0010).

```text
Planned --> Scheduled --> Pending risk check --> Ready
--> Submitted --> Acknowledged --> Partially filled --> Filled
```

Terminal alternatives: Canceled, Rejected, Expired, Failed. Broker statuses normalise into this model and the raw status is kept.

### 12.6 Reconciliation

Every 60 seconds, and at once when the broker reports an unknown order, reconciliation compares app orders, broker orders, fills and positions. Updates it finds are applied and audited as healed; missing orders, differing positions and unknown orders open a mismatch incident (one per kind, runtime and account) and pause the affected strategies. Data incidents resolve themselves; mismatches and repeated rejects are resolved by a person from the Orders banner (C17, ADR 0014).

### 12.7 The Orders tab

- **Strategies on paper** cards: each strategy's S number and name, with the run status (Running, Paused, Stopped) and "Just started" on a line under them (N246, N247).
- Views: all, working, filled, canceled or rejected, scheduled, with counts; filters by strategy, run, cycle, phase, instrument, account and dates in the viewer's time zone, kept in the address so a view can be bookmarked (saved views are bookmarks, D7).
- A dense order table, a scheduled-legs table, a positions table with manual close (stopped strategies only), the kill switch, and a banner for paused strategies and open incidents with Resolve.
- The order drawer shows the full event timeline, the source rule, trigger values and the broker's raw status.
- Wherever Orders names a strategy (cards, banner, tables, positions, drawer, Strategy filter), its S number comes before the name (N246).
- Updates stream by server-sent events from `pg_notify`, and the tab also refreshes every 30 s; no manual "process next feed update" is needed (C27).

## 13. Performance

Performance comes from reconciled fills and account data, not from app-submitted orders. The method is also explained in the app at `/performance/methodology` (C25, ADRs 0019 to 0021).

- **Views and filters**: dates (NYSE sessions, inclusive), account, strategy, version, asset class (only the classes the person has traded, N43), mode (Live is empty until live trading). KPI cards, equity and drawdown charts, strategy and asset contribution, the trade/cycle table, and data-quality notes. A strategy's page links here filtered to it.
- **Matching**: fills are matched first in, first out within a book (one runtime, account and instrument). One trade is one strategy cycle, with each leg's contribution inside it.
- **Valuation**: every account is valued every 5 minutes at the last price from the tenant's routed feed; a mark older than 60 s is stale and counted. The first valuation at or after the 4:00 pm New York close is final.
- **Returns**: accounts show a daily time-weighted return on equity, with Sharpe annualised over 252 sessions at a zero risk-free rate (not shown under 20 sessions). Filtered views show P&L in dollars, drawdown in dollars and return on peak capital.
- **Fees** as the broker reports them per fill (none on paper). USD only, labelled.
- **Broker differences**: at each close both sides' positions are snapshotted; any quantity difference, or equity more than $1.00 apart, is flagged with an always-shown notice and the broker's equity is the figure that counts. Positions the app cannot explain appear under "Outside the app".
- The built-in paper account starts at $100,000. Deposits and withdrawals at a real broker are not tracked yet and show as equity differences.

### 14.7 Patterns (pattern discovery)

Pattern discovery searches the point-in-time factor store for simple rules that preceded large moves, and verifies them out of sample (ADR 0091):

- **New scan** form: universe, spike or plunge, move X %, horizon H trading days, factor categories, depth, minimum support, optional date range. The web app writes the `pattern_scan` row; the miner claims it at its next batch run (daily, so a scan can wait up to a day) (N268, O170).
- **Labels and atoms.** Every (day, instrument) is labelled by whether the price reached +X % (spike) or −X % (plunge) within H trading days from that close, from daily highs and lows chained from stored bar ratios (O160). Atoms are top or bottom quintile across the universe (with 50+ known values that day) or own-history 90th/10th percentile (252 trailing known values, 120+ required). Options factors and the T-bill rate offer own-history atoms only (O163) (N266).
- **Search.** Beam search over conjunctions up to the chosen depth (beam 200 by Wilson lower bound; a child kept only when it beats its best parent). The 50 best finalists, after dropping any sharing 80 %+ occurrences with a better one, are written with every occurrence in one transaction (N266).
- **Verification gate.** The range is split 70/30 by trading days; an occurrence counts in a window only when its whole horizon lies inside it. **Verified** needs holdout n ≥ 15, a holdout hit-rate Wilson 95 % lower bound above the holdout base rate, lift ≥ 1.25, and a one-sided binomial p-value surviving Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.10 across the scan's finalists; otherwise **Candidate** with the failed checks listed. Rules tested is shown beside every pattern. A scan that verifies nothing is a valid result. The p-value assumes independent occurrences, so the 10 % false-discovery rate is a floor, and the pages say so (N267, O171).
- **Pages.** The scan page shows rules tested, finalists, verified, the discovery and holdout ranges and base rates, and patterns in plain words with hit rate, base rate and lift. The pattern page shows evidence by window (holdout, since verification, re-verification, discovery), an event study (mean move from −10 to H+5 days, ±1 standard error), the occurrences, and the survivorship and mass-testing notes. The Patterns tab also shows the factor **coverage report** (N261, N268).
- **Promote to the Theory Library.** One button creates one theory (T, daily, stocks; category multi-factor; maturity Researched) whose name is the rule in words and whose description carries the evidence; the pattern links to it. A Candidate can be promoted after a confirmation. A pattern is promoted once; no strategy is created (O159) (N269).
- **Decay monitoring.** Each batch run re-scores every Verified pattern not scored in 7 days on occurrences after its holdout: with 10+ fresh occurrences it is **Broken** (fresh hit rate at or below the base rate), **Weakening** (beats the base rate but its Wilson bound does not, or lift < 1.1) or **Holding**. "Re-verify now" runs the gate on everything after the discovery window. Stored evidence is never rewritten; re-verification is stored beside it (N270, O172).

The factor store and miner are described in §16.7.

## 16. Data sources and background jobs

All of these are global public data, written only by the worker's login and read by the app where noted.

### 16.1 Market data and instruments

Instruments are canonical records (symbol and venue, asset class, currency, provider ids, `kind` `tradable` or `indicator_only`; indices have asset class `index` and are always indicator-only), never keyed by ticker alone; option and futures fields exist in the schema for later. Shortability is stored from Alpaca at lookup (unknown for older lookups, O27) (N30, N41). The app keeps no bar store: bars come from the feeds on demand, cached per runtime (§12.2) and in short-lived caches (`price_chart`, custom-feed cache) (O152).

### 16.2 Options recording

- **Universe**: the seed list (SPY, QQQ, DIA, IWM, AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, AMZN, META, GOOGL, TSLA, AMD, NFLX, AVGO) plus every US stock or ETF a saved strategy uses; nobody adds one by hand. Saving a strategy adds its stocks and ETFs; their data starts with the next recorded session (N50, N56).
- **Recording**: from 16:45 New York on each NYSE day, the worker fetches each underlying's delayed chain (`CBOE_OPTIONS_URL/{SYMBOL}.json`; 20 s timeout, two retries, one more attempt 30 minutes later). A missed day is reported, never filled. A file stamped before the previous session's close is refused; one before the day's own close is stored with a note (O33). Rows with impossible values are skipped and counted (N51).
- **Pricing**: the app computes its own IV and greeks: Black–Scholes–Merton with a continuous dividend yield, forward F = S·e^((r−q)T), calendar days ÷ 365, IV by Newton's method from the Brenner–Subrahmanyam estimate with a bisection fallback on 0.0001 to 5, out-of-the-money quotes only (bid > 0, ask > bid, spread ≤ 40 % of the mid), ATM strike nearest the forward, inputs the Alpaca daily close, FRED DTB3 and the stock's point-in-time dividend yield (0 for ETFs). CBOE's IV and greeks are stored, never used. Signals wait (up to 10 days) for the close and rate (N52, `packages/domain/src/options`).
- **History rebuild**: once per underlying, daily signals back to February 2024 from Alpaca's daily option bars (Friday and monthly expiries, strikes within 15 % of the close, contracts that traded 10+ times that day), stored as `rebuilt` (feed `alpaca_bars`), approximate, without open-interest, quote or volume signals (N54, O34).
- **Retention** 400 days (`OPTIONS_HISTORY_DAYS`). **Health**: Down once no underlying has been stored for 3 trading days.
- **Key**: `OPTIONS_ALPACA_KEY_ID` / `OPTIONS_ALPACA_SECRET_KEY`, else the oldest platform admin's active Alpaca paper feed key (read at start); never a member's key. `OPTIONS_RECORDING=off` stops it (N59, N85, ADR 0050, O61).

### 16.3 Company filings

Every value a company's filings support is stored as a point-in-time observation (company CIK, field, value, period end, available from the EDGAR acceptance time, accession, form; 17:30 New York on the filing date when the time is unknown), as originally filed: a restatement is a new observation, nothing is overwritten. A bar at D reads the latest observation available by D, and none when its period ended over 15 months before. Observations are built by replaying each company's 10-K and 10-Q filings in acceptance order (TTM = the fiscal year just ended, else the last four quarters). Splits in filings are confirmed by the cover-page share count; tagging gaps are worked around as recorded in N49 (a missing dividend quarter, payment-date dividends, EPS from net income ÷ shares, balance-sheet shares, share-class siblings). Filings are fetched on demand and refreshed after a day; the derived fields `ocf_ttm` and `capex_ttm` feed the pattern factors (N34, N39, N46, N48, N49, N261, ADR 0043).

### 16.4 Earnings, deals and rates

- **Earnings releases** (`earnings_release`, `earnings_sue`): each evening after 18:30 New York the worker reads new 8-K Item 2.02 filings for the S&P 500 plus the stocks a strategy uses, stores the release at its acceptance time, and completes EPS and SUE from the 10-Q or 10-K once filed (looked for 120 days). EDGAR is the record; a calendar feed never overwrites a release. A one-off rebuild back to 1 February 2024 marks rows `rebuilt`. 10 requests a second with a contact User-Agent (`SEC_CONTACT_EMAIL` or the admin's SEC EDGAR feed) (N135, N136, N139, O111).
- **Earnings calendar** (`earnings_calendar`): scheduled dates from FMP or Finnhub, first and last seen (§15.2).
- **Deal tracker** (`deal_event`, ids D00001… gapless in announcement order; append-only `deal_status_history`; `deal_filing`): found from EDGAR full-text search for merger agreements in 8-Ks and tender offers (SC 14D9); status from the target's later filings (proxy or 425 → pending; 8-K Item 2.01 or delisting → closed; Item 1.02 terminating → withdrawn; closed and withdrawn are final). Rows sharing the target, announcement date and offer are one deal; the higher-confidence parse is kept and the other merged into it. The worker stores each active deal's latest daily close once a day and after the evening close (N140, N151, N152, O113, O118).
- **Rates** (`rates_daily`): each DTB3 value the options inputs already fetch, as a fraction (N142).
- `event_scan_state` records how far each job has read.

### 16.5 Alpaca request budget

Every Alpaca client in the worker counts requests per key over the last minute. Foreground work (strategies, screens, charts, searches) goes at once. Background work (the options recording and closes, 5-minute back-test bar loads) waits until it has sent fewer than 60 in the minute, the key fewer than 120 (80 of Alpaca's 200 kept for foreground), and any 429 on the key has passed; a floor of 10 background requests a minute still goes while the key is under 200. The budget is per worker process (O70) (N96, ADR 0052).

### 16.6 ML prediction signals

- **Model series** (`ml_model`; first `mf-ranker-ridge` on the S&P 500) are trained outside the request path; each refit (`ml_model_run`) serves one monthly prediction date and writes one score per member (`ml_prediction`). Predictions are immutable (triggers refuse update and delete); a bad run is superseded and replaced, and superseded runs are never read. A series may name a theory; saving or copying a strategy never creates a model. Series are platform-wide (per-user series are open, O151) (N239, ADR 0086).
- **Feature snapshots and labels** (worker job, on unless `ML_SNAPSHOTS=off`): for each prediction date (the last trading day of each month), resolve the universe as a back test would and compute the eleven v1 features as of that close through the back test's own market view (registry metrics on daily bars, the EDGAR replay, the events store), so a feature equals what a condition read that day. Missing means null. Each feature is winsorised at the 1st/99th percentile and z-scored (sample SD); the earliest date with 90 % coverage is reported per feature (O150). Once a date's 21 trading days have passed, each member's realised forward return is stored (`ml_label`, O152). Uses the sharing admin's feeds; chunks of 24 dates; resumable; RSI history always loads from the model's first date (O153) (N242).
- **Trainer** (`apps/ml-trainer`, Python and scikit-learn, off unless `ML_TRAINER_ENABLED=on`, run by Railway cron, O149): for every prediction date T, ridge regression on the z-scored features → label (21-day forward return minus the universe median), using only cross-sections whose label window ended before T (an embargo), from the latest feature-availability date, expanding window, at least 18 cross-sections; alpha from {0.1, 1, 10, 100} by the best mean Spearman IC on the last six eligible cross-sections. Each refit records its window, `max_label_end`, alpha and coefficients; members with a null feature get no score. One command catches every model up date by date, so backfill and the monthly run are the same (N243, N244).
- **Evaluation**: once labels realise, rank IC, decile spread and coverage are stored (`ml_evaluation`), and a Markdown report per series (`ml_report`) gives the monthly IC series, decile spread, coverage, the binding feature, the latest coefficients and the survivorship caveat (N244). Yong's review of the first results (the report and the S00035-SM experiment, 2026-11-13) is still owed; until then the model keeps scoring monthly on its own (§25).
- **Use**: the `ml.score` metric (§8.7.1) with model provenance on the cycle card (§11.3).

### 16.7 Pattern factor store and miner

- **Factor store** (`factor_snapshot`, insert-only): for every member of the snapshot groups (S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, Dow 30, S&P 400, S&P 600) and the options universe, one row per trading day from `PATTERN_SNAPSHOT_FROM` (default 2016-01-04), then each day after the close: raw factor values (unknown ones left out) and the day's bar as ratios to the previous adjusted close. Values come only from the back test's market view as of that close: registry metrics, filings as filed (plus `revenue_growth_yoy`, `fcf_margin`), events, options signals, and session values from 5-minute bars for the options universe only (O158). Daily passes load 800 days of bars (O162). Catalogue v1: `packages/domain/src/patterns/factors.ts`; each factor declares its atom modes. `factor_coverage` keeps known values per factor and day. Kill switch `PATTERN_SNAPSHOT_ENABLED` (unset means on in Railway staging only, O161) (N261, ADR 0091).
- **Miner** (`apps/ml-trainer`, `miner/`): claims queued `pattern_scan` rows (skip-locked), labels, searches, verifies and writes `pattern` and `pattern_occurrence` rows (§14.7). Kill switch `PATTERN_MINER_ENABLED` (off until the backfill review) (N266).

### 17.2 Separation of concerns

The rule engine evaluates normalised data and definitions; feed adapters translate provider data into a canonical model; broker adapters translate intents and normalise updates; the execution orchestrator owns cycle state and timing; the risk engine approves or rejects each outbound order; the order ledger is the source of truth for app-side order history; the performance engine consumes reconciled fills and positions. ### 19.3 MCP endpoint for AI agents

- `/api/mcp` serves MCP over Streamable HTTP with stateless JSON replies (`initialize`, `ping`, `tools/list`, `tools/call`), behind `AGENT_API_ENABLED` (N263, ADR 0092).
- **Credentials**: `Authorization: Bearer` with an agent token (`tma_…`, 256 bits, SHA-256 hash stored, optional expiry) or an OAuth access token. OAuth 2.1 per the MCP authorization spec: protected-resource and authorization-server metadata under `/.well-known/`, dynamic client registration, `/oauth/authorize` behind the normal sign-in, a consent screen naming the agent with Read only (default) or Read and operate (only if asked for), authorization code + PKCE S256, 1-hour access tokens, rotating 30-day refresh tokens, revocation. Unknown, revoked, expired or closed-account credentials get 401 with a JSON-RPC error. 60 calls a minute per connection.
- **Read tools**: `search_theories`, `get_theory`, `list_strategies`, `get_strategy` (definition, plain-words summary, validation), `list_backtests`, `get_backtest` (metrics, cycles with Why lines), `screen_stocks` (waits up to 20 s, else returns `pending` with the screen id, O168), `get_paper_accounts`, `get_screen_context` (the Ask AI snapshot).
- **Operate tools**: `create_strategy` (the builder's save path; returns the S number and validation; an invalid definition creates nothing and returns the app's errors), `update_strategy`, `run_backtest`, `create_theory` (registers at once, since the Library has no drafts, O165). A read connection sees them but gets `insufficient_scope` with a message on how to reconnect (O166).
- **Boundary**: no tool, at any scope, starts or stops execution, touches orders, shares, billing, credentials, data feeds, invites or users, or deletes anything; a snapshot test pins the list (N264).

## 20. Security, privacy and compliance

### 20.1 Security

- TLS in transit, managed encryption at rest, strict tenant isolation (§4.1) and least-privilege roles: owner (migrations), `tm_app` (under row-level security), `tm_auth` (auth tables only), and `tm_secrets`, held only by the worker's login, for sealed credentials and global data writes (C36).
- **Credentials** are sealed in Postgres: a fresh AES-256-GCM key per secret, wrapped with an RSA-OAEP public key whose private half is held in **AWS KMS** and used only by the worker; the web app refuses to start with a private key; the web role can write sealed columns but never read them (C23, ADR 0015, ADR 0035). Invite codes and Ask AI agent keys are sealed with a key derived from the web app's own secret instead, because the web app must open them; rotating that secret makes everyone re-enter agent keys (ADR 0085, O175).
- CSRF defence: SameSite=Lax cookies plus an Origin check on every mutating request. Rate limits shared through Postgres (§4.4). Better Auth's trusted proxies are set (D5).
- Custom REST feeds may reach only public https hosts (§15.2). Only the app may frame its pages (§5.4).
- CI runs `pnpm audit --prod`, Gitleaks over the repository and its history, and Trivy over the runtime image and deploy files. Stripe keys must be test keys; the Alpaca client refuses any host but the paper endpoint. `docs/security-review.md` lists what protects each boundary and the accepted risks.

### 20.2 Audit

Append-only, secrets redacted. Audited: strategy create, edit, version and status changes; back-test starts and provenance; feed, route and broker changes, failovers and switch-backs; credential saves (by fingerprint, never value); start, stop, pauses and resumes; triggers that generate orders; risk approvals and rejections; order submission, cancellation and reconciliation; incidents; billing and plan changes; invite use, minting and release (count and note, never the code); admin actions (lock, unlock, password reset, invites); shared-feeds switch; agent connections created and revoked (C24, N16, N25, N237, N265).

**Agent activity** (`agent_call_log`): every tool call an agent made, refused ones included, with time, connection, tool, an argument summary (secret-looking fields left out), outcome and error class, and duration; shown in Settings › AI agents › Activity, kept 90 days, removed by the account purge. Calls with an unknown credential name no person and are not logged (O167).

### 20.3 Notifications

In-app only, each with a dedupe key and a link to where to look: strategy start and stop, triggers, rejections, partial and full fills, disconnects, stale data, risk pauses, reconciliation mismatches, feed down, failover and switch-back, plan changes and grace reminders, closing-time broker differences, and sharing (received, accepted, declined). Email and push are not built (C24, N122).

### 20.4 Compliance posture and the launch gate

The app states that it is a user-directed trading tool and that back tests do not guarantee results (sign-up, every page's footer, the top of the placeholder Terms); Terms and Privacy say no lawyer has reviewed them. Before public launch or real money, the launch gate (`docs/launch-checks.md`) requires:

- Legal review of brokerage API terms, market-data licensing, disclosures, record retention, regional availability and any advisory obligations.
- Cboe's display terms for the VIX (`CBOE_DISPLAY_TERMS`) and for options data (`CBOE_OPTIONS_DISPLAY_TERMS`, covering Research's computed chain) for a paid service (N45, N58, N65).
- Whether Alpaca's terms allow values computed through the app's or admin's key (options rebuild, shared admin feeds) to be shown to other users; a separate app key before public launch (O36, O61, N237).
- FMP and Finnhub terms re-checked before charging subscribers, or the calendar set to `none` (O110).
- Custom feeds: each person is responsible for their provider's terms (N23).

Raw CBOE quotes, IVs and greeks never leave the worker; Research shows the app's computed values with open interest and volume (N65). Whether showing CBOE's open interest and volume is acceptable is still open (O45) and is part of the Cboe terms review above.

## 21. Non-functional requirements

### 21.1 Correctness

Money uses decimal arithmetic (decimal.js and Postgres `numeric`, carried as strings); research chart prices are plain numbers because they are only drawn. Quantities obey instrument precision. External events keep provider time, receipt time and source. Definitions are versioned with migration on read. Calculations are deterministic for identical versioned inputs; back-test results carry an engine version. The regression corpus re-runs every fixture back test (the three SPY strategies Trend SMA200, Mean Reversion RSI-2 and Breakout Donchian 20/10, a two-leg strategy, and every acceptance fixture) through every schema migration with identical decisions, orders, fills and metrics (N15, N84, N214).

### 21.2 Performance

Load target (approved 2026-10-05): 20 users with 5 running strategies each and 10 back tests at once; pages p95 under 1 s; orders placed within 5 s of their trigger. A k6 script is in `load/`; it has not yet been run against staging. Back tests are sized up front (§11.2); paper runs keep bars between passes (§12.2). No high-frequency trading.

### 21.3 Availability and recovery

Designed, not yet running: nightly `pg_dump` to Cloudflare R2 at 07:30 UTC, kept 30 days, with `restore-check.sh` as a monthly runbook step. Recovery objectives: **RPO 24 hours, RTO 4 hours** (`docs/deploy.md`). Runbooks in `docs/runbooks`: incidents, backups and restore, key rotation, billing webhooks, a stuck worker, feed or broker trouble. The R2 backups, restore drill and uptime monitor on staging wait on Yong's accounts (`docs/launch-checks.md`).

### 21.4 Testing

Unit tests for expressions, indicators, grouping, timing, sizing and risk; property tests for order states and numeric edges; integration tests for the database, queues, webhooks and adapter contracts; end-to-end tests for sign-up through Performance, with axe accessibility checks (WCAG 2 A/AA) on every page and a layout test at 1440, 1728 and 1920 px. Adapters are tested against fakes transcribed from each provider's API reference (Alpaca, Stripe, SEC EDGAR, CBOE, FRED, OpenAI-compatible), checked against fixtures; the broker contract suite also runs with injected duplicates, reordering, lost replies and disconnects; a manual check with a real key confirms each provider (C38, D10). Automated tests run on the invented DEMO-\* instruments and demo feed, switched on only by `DEMO_DATA=on` on a local test server (ignored on Railway or any non-local URL) (N43, O31). Paper trading must pass a sustained validation period before live trading.

## 23. Acceptance criteria for v1

Every criterion below is marked Done in `docs/traceability.md` with its tests; acceptance tests use DEMO-A and DEMO-B in place of PEP and KO, because seed data is generic.

- **Strategies**: a signed-in user can create, save, reopen, rename, copy, archive and delete strategies; they persist across sessions and devices; nobody can reach another user's strategy through the UI or API; a running strategy keeps the exact immutable version that generated each order.
- **Multi-asset rules**: a condition can compare an indicator for PEP with one for KO, or with a fixed value; nested AND/OR/NOT logic saves, reloads, validates and evaluates consistently; different assets may use different fields, intervals and parameters.
- **Multi-asset orders**: one Entry can long one asset and short another; each leg stores instrument, account, side, quantity, type and timing; unsupported broker capabilities block Start with a useful message; partial fills and one-leg failures are represented and auditable.
- **Timing**: immediate, exact zoned time, or a delay from a durable reference event; "five minutes after Entry order submitted" survives a restart; a retry cannot duplicate a broker order; market-closed handling follows the strategy's policy.
- **Back test**: runs only on request; identifies version, data range, assumptions and engine version; aligns assets without lookahead; shows overall and per-leg outcomes; warns when the strategy changed after the latest test.
- **Execution and Orders**: Start sits after Back test with mode-appropriate confirmation; running paper strategies consume feed data automatically; Entry legs become intents only after Entry qualifies; Exit follows the Entry milestone; orders show source strategy, version, cycle, phase, trigger and broker timeline; Stop never silently cancels or liquidates.
- **Settings**: feeds can be viewed, added, tested, turned on and off and removed; routes name primary and backup; several platforms and paper accounts can be connected; secrets never return to the browser; turning off an in-use integration pauses or reroutes safely.
- **Multi-user subscription**: sign-up, subscribe and plan-gated features; webhook replays change nothing twice; limits enforced on the server; plan changes never create unsafe, silent execution.

Later features carry their own acceptance checks in their briefs and ADRs (for example the Theory Library's A1 to A10, the intraday pack's three strategies S00032-EN ORB, S00033-EN VWAP and S00034-ED gap, and the AI-agent live acceptance), recorded in `v1.2-deviations.md` and `docs/traceability.md`.

**Definition of done for the first usable production release**: a subscriber can securely sign in, create and keep multi-asset strategies, run reproducible back tests, configure a data feed and paper account, start a tested strategy, watch Entry and Exit orders generated automatically with timing rules, inspect the full order lifecycle and review performance, with tenant isolation, audit, risk controls and restart recovery. All of this is built on paper; what remains is production hosting and the launch gate (§22.2).
